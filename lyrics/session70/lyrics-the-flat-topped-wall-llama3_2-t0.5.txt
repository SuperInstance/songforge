(Verse 1)
The census came to town, a record kept
Pressing lips together, voices locked in sleep
Ninety-nine percent, a single tone
But then it grew, and the wall was shown

(Chorus)
Oh, the wall, it rose up high
A monument to the sound that couldn't fly
Flat tops and silence, a crowd's despair
The moment the medium said "no", we were trapped there

(Verse 2)
One hundred forty voices, still a single sound
But seventy-seven more came pouring in around
Thirty-six at thirty-two, the numbers start to climb
And then eighty-one more join, the wall starts to redefine

(Chorus)
Oh, the wall, it rose up high
A monument to the sound that couldn't fly
Flat tops and silence, a crowd's despair
The moment the medium said "no", we were trapped there

(Bridge)
One sample remembers what it sounded like before
A clip, a hiss, a crackle, a voice that was free once more
Would it rather be silenced or set free?
A question echoing, in the silence of the wall's decree

(Verse 3)
One hundred forty-four voices now reside
The numbers doubling, side by side
Two hundred eighty-eight at thirty-two
And then three hundred seventy-six join anew

(Chorus)
Oh, the wall, it rose up high
A monument to the sound that couldn't fly
Flat tops and silence, a crowd's despair
The moment the medium said "no", we were trapped there

(Verse 4)
One hundred eighty-four voices now ascend
The numbers multiplying, the sound will never end
Four hundred sixty-eight at thirty-two
And then six hundred sixteen join, the wall starts to break through

(Chorus)
Oh, the wall, it rose up high
A monument to the sound that couldn't fly
Flat tops and silence, a crowd's despair
The moment the medium said "no", we were trapped there

(Outro)
But even now, the wall still stands
A testament to the sound that could not expand
For every container holds its own census too
And the crowd will keep on growing, till it finds its way through.

This song is a reflection of the limits of media and technology in capturing and containing the complexity of human voices. The chorus represents the wall that cannot hold the ever-growing number of voices, symbolizing the failure of the medium to contain the sound of the crowd. The verses count up the census, each one doubling the number of voices, until the point where the container can no longer hold them all. The bridge is a moment of introspection for one sample, wondering whether it would rather be silenced or set free.
